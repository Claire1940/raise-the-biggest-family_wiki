### What to Review Before Adoption

Use the following comparison to organize your decision. The game may present different labels or requirements as your progression changes, so focus on the underlying choice rather than memorizing one fixed route.

| Checkpoint | Why It Matters | Recommended Approach |
|---|---|---|
| Household capacity | More family members increase daily demands | Adopt only when existing needs are under control |
| Available care | Dependents need reliable attention and support | Confirm that another family member or service can help |
| Resource reserve | Growth can place pressure on supplies and currency | Keep a reserve instead of spending everything immediately |
| Current objectives | Major tasks may compete with adoption responsibilities | Finish urgent milestones before expanding |
| Family balance | Repeating one type of choice can create weak coverage | Mix different strengths and responsibilities |

A useful rule is to separate **short-term convenience** from **long-term value**. An adoption that appears easy at first may require more attention later. Conversely, a child with higher early demands may become a valuable part of the household once properly supported.

### Early Adoption Priorities

New players should generally prioritize:

- A manageable household size.
- Dependable daily support.
- Clear responsibility assignments.
- Enough resources for unexpected needs.
- A family structure that supports future goals.

Avoid judging an adoption by appearance, immediate cost, or one visible statistic alone. The strongest choice is usually the one that fits your current plan and remains useful as the family grows.

<Section icon={Users} title="How to Choose the Right Adoption">

Adoption choices should match the type of family you are building. Some players want fast population growth, while others prefer a smaller household with more focused development. Neither style is automatically better. The important factor is consistency between your adoption choice and your available support.

Start by identifying your household’s biggest weakness. If you lack time, choose an option that does not create an immediate care burden. If your family has enough support but lacks future flexibility, prioritize a child who can broaden the household’s responsibilities. If resources are limited, delay expansion until your economy is more dependable.

<Callout type="warning" title="Avoid Overexpansion">
Adding several family members in quick succession can make every household task harder to manage. Expand one stage at a time and check whether your routine remains sustainable.
</Callout>

### Adoption Profiles

| Adoption Profile | Best For | Main Risk | Planning Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low-maintenance choice | New households and limited schedules | Slower early development | Use spare time for household upgrades |
| High-potential choice | Established families with strong support | Greater care requirements | Assign dedicated support before adoption |
| Balanced choice | Flexible, general-purpose households | No single standout advantage | Build complementary family roles |
| Challenge choice | Experienced players seeking difficulty | Mistakes affect the entire household | Keep extra resources and backup care |

These profiles are planning categories, not fixed character rankings. The right choice depends on the state of your household when the adoption becomes available.

### Compare the Whole Household

Before confirming, ask four questions:

1. **Who will provide daily care?**  
   Do not assume your main character can handle every responsibility while also completing other objectives.

2. **What changes immediately?**  
   Check whether the adoption affects available space, expenses, attention, or progression requirements.

3. **What happens if priorities shift?**  
   A good choice should remain manageable if a new task, event, or family requirement appears.

4. **Does this choice fill a gap?**  
   If your household already has several members with similar strengths, choose an option that improves flexibility instead of repeating the same role.

### Efficient Growth Pattern

A measured growth pattern works well for most playstyles:

- Expand once.
- Complete the new care and household tasks.
- Rebuild your reserve.
- Review the family’s strengths and weaknesses.
- Expand again only when the routine is stable.

This approach may feel slower than selecting every available option, but it reduces the chance of creating a household that is large without being functional.

<Section icon={Shield} title="Common Mistakes and Family Management">

Most adoption problems come from poor timing rather than a single bad choice. Players often focus on the benefit of a new family member and overlook the workload created by that decision. A strong household plan accounts for both sides.

<Callout type="warning" title="Mistake to Avoid">
Do not spend every available resource on adoption and leave nothing for care, emergencies, or progression tasks. Keep a reserve whenever possible.
</Callout>

### Mistakes That Slow Progress

| Mistake | What It Causes | Better Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Adopting during a shortage | Basic household needs compete for resources | Restore your reserve first |
| Choosing by one trait | The family becomes unbalanced | Compare overall fit and future usefulness |
| Ignoring care assignments | Existing members receive less attention | Define responsibilities before expansion |
| Expanding repeatedly | Daily tasks become difficult to maintain | Use a staged growth cycle |
| Neglecting adjustment time | New problems appear before the last one is solved | Review the household after each adoption |

### Household Management Priorities

When the family becomes more demanding, organize tasks in this order:

1. **Essential care**  
   Handle immediate needs before optional activities or low-priority upgrades.

2. **Resource maintenance**  
   Keep the household supplied and avoid spending reserves on cosmetic or nonessential choices.

3. **Responsibility coverage**  
   Make sure each recurring task has a clear owner or support method.

4. **Progression planning**  
   Resume expansion and larger objectives only after the household is stable.

5. **Long-term balance**  
   Review whether the family still has a useful mix of strengths and responsibilities.

### Building a Balanced Family

A balanced family usually benefits from varied responsibilities rather than repeated specialization. Consider whether your current household already has enough support for care, progression, resource gathering, and future objectives. If one area is weak, use the next adoption to improve that area.

Do not treat balance as a requirement to make every family member identical. A household can include different strengths while still working effectively. The goal is to create complementary roles that reduce pressure on the main character and make future decisions easier.
