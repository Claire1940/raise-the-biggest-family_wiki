### How to Recognize a Productive Route

A productive apple route has three characteristics:

- It contains several possible tree or pickup locations.
- It can be completed without unnecessary detours.
- It returns close to your storage, family base, or next objective.

If a route contains only one tree and requires a long journey, keep it as a backup location rather than your main farming path. A route with fewer individual nodes can still be better when the nodes are tightly grouped.

<Callout type="info" title="Route Planning">
Your best apple route is not always the location with the most trees. Prioritize the route that lets you harvest, return, store, and repeat with minimal downtime.
</Callout>

### When an Apple Tree Seems Empty

An empty-looking tree does not always mean the route is incorrect. Move around the node, change the camera angle, and check whether the interaction prompt appears from another position. If no prompt or pickup is available, leave the location and continue the route rather than standing still.

After completing the loop, return later and check the same trees again. This makes it easier to determine whether the location is temporary, requires a reset, or simply has a limited collection state.

### Better Camera Control

When approaching a tree, slow down instead of sprinting directly through it. Move along the outside edge, turn the camera toward the trunk and ground, and watch for an interaction indicator. This method is especially useful in dense areas where foliage or structures can obscure the pickup.

### Better Route Memory

Use landmarks rather than trying to remember every tree individually. A fence corner, bridge, building entrance, or fork in the road can mark the beginning of a route segment. Divide the full loop into two or three sections and complete them in the same order.

### Better Time Management

Do not wait indefinitely at one empty location. If a tree has no available pickup, continue with another task or check a backup zone. Returning later gives you more useful information than repeatedly interacting with the same empty spot.
