<Section icon={Map} title="How to Organize New Feature Updates">

A well-organized update page lets readers scan the newest information without losing older context. Start with the date, then identify the feature type, summarize the change, and explain its practical effect. Keep each entry focused on one subject rather than combining unrelated additions into a long paragraph.

A consistent layout also makes future editing faster. When another update arrives, contributors can add a new row or subsection without rewriting the entire article.

<Steps>
  <Step num="1" title="Identify the update">
    Record the feature name and describe the change in one sentence. Avoid promotional wording and do not add details that have not been documented.
  </Step>
  <Step num="2" title="Assign a category">
    Place the feature under content, systems, quality of life, story, or community tools. If it fits multiple categories, choose the one that best explains its main purpose.
  </Step>
  <Step num="3" title="Add the publication date">
    Use the format 2026-10-31 or the verified date connected to the announcement. Do not substitute an estimated date for a confirmed one.
  </Step>
  <Step num="4" title="Apply a status label">
    Mark the entry as confirmed, previewed, reported, or unconfirmed. Update the label if stronger evidence becomes available.
  </Step>
  <Step num="5" title="Review the wording">
    Check that the summary is factual, concise, and easy to understand. Remove predictions that could be mistaken for official information.
  </Step>
</Steps>

| Feature name | Use the clearest known name | Inventing a formal title |
| Date | Use a verified 2026 date | Using a prediction as a launch date |

<Section icon={Shield} title="Reliable Update Tracking in 2026">

The strongest fan-wiki workflow uses a source hierarchy. Official announcements and published update notes should receive the highest priority. Public previews may support a “Previewed” label, while community discussion should remain clearly separated from confirmed information.

Because update details can change, every entry should be easy to revise. Avoid wording that depends on temporary assumptions, unannounced schedules, or unsupported numbers.

| Evidence level | Use on the wiki | Language pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Officially confirmed | Main feature list and dated changelog | “The update adds…” |
| Official preview | Preview section | “The feature has been shown as…” |
| Reliable report | Reported section with attribution | “A report describes…” |
| Community discussion | Discussion or speculation section | “Some fans suggest…” |
| No reliable confirmation | Do not present as a feature | “Not confirmed” |
